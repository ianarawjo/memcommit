The explicit defaults are to format document titles in sentence case and to italicize titles of works consistently throughout a document. A named style guide may override only the corresponding default: title capitalization or work-title formatting. It cannot override the requirement to retain “for” when intended. [1] [2]

The user-request conditions are: when asked “How does this read?”, give an opinion and first assess sentence order and paragraph division rather than editing immediately; when later asked to polish, preserve the overall structure and citation-needed markers and change only problematic wording; when asked to change one expression, leave almost everything else—including selected technical or project-specific terms—unchanged; and when asked only for review, report a citation-verification problem before rewriting. [3] [4] [5] [6]

Other task-triggered conditions apply when their stated circumstances arise: preserve all four intended points while removing redundancy; for a shorter fixed limit, target roughly a 20–30% cut from redundant or unnecessary material without making claims more categorical; use an accurate connector when the next idea is merely related; preserve the specified terminology for titles about interaction with AI agent memory; investigate the complete original source when content is said to look wrong; and verify complete sources before adding or reusing citations or references. [7] [8] [9] [10] [11] [12] [6]
