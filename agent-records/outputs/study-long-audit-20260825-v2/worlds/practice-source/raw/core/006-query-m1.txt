A reviewer must preserve the draft’s overall structure and citation-needed markers during polishing; retain almost everything else—including selected technical or project-specific terms—when changing one expression; keep every intended point; and preserve the original strength and conditions of claims when shortening. [1] [2] [3] [4]

They must also preserve exact intended terminology in titles, including “interaction,” “management,” “AI agent memory,” and “for” when intended. [5] [6]

They must avoid editing when asked only for an opinion such as “How does this read?” and should first assess sentence order and paragraph division. If a review uncovers a citation-verification problem, they must report it before rewriting rather than silently editing the draft. [7] [8]
